# The AI Data-Center Build-Out: Bubble Mechanics, Physical Bottlenecks, And Cost Transmission

_A ScienceForge research report on infrastructure constraints and cost transmission_

**Revision:** September 26, 2026 | focused evidence and editorial update
**Status:** Updated research manuscript; historical model results retained.

## Abstract
This report examines how genuine demand for AI infrastructure can coexist with equipment constraints, financing risk and uneven local electricity costs. The September update adds dated primary-source evidence and corrects the interpretation of historical scenario weights. Earlier Forge model classifications remain exploratory; the retained market backtest does not establish selection skill. The useful test is whether operating demand, delivered infrastructure and cost recovery develop together.

## September 26 2026 evidence update

**Why this matters:** More computing can support useful services and scientific work. Building the infrastructure also creates decisions about electricity, land and money. Residents need to know who pays for upgrades; businesses need to know whether promised capacity can actually be delivered.

The [IEA's April 2026 assessment](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary) reports that global data-center electricity consumption grew 17% in 2025. Its central projection rises from 485 TWh in 2025 to 950 TWh in 2030. The 2030 figure is a projection, not measured consumption. The same assessment explains that efficiency improvements can be offset by wider use and more demanding applications. For local electricity bills, capacity constraints and cost allocation matter: demand growth can raise costs in constrained systems, while spare capacity can change the result.

Virginia provides a concrete example of policy design. The [SCC's February 2026 fact sheet](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/media/sccvirginiagov-home/about-the-scc/fact-sheets/scc-data-center-initiatives-02-2026.pdf) describes a separate GS-5 class for qualifying Dominion large-load customers, minimum charges and collateral provisions. New qualifying customers contracting from January 1, 2027 face a 14-year service obligation. These are protections intended to limit cost shifting; their existence is not evidence that every household has already been protected from every increase.

These sources support continued investigation of physical constraints and regional cost allocation. They do not validate the Forge's historical market scores, ticker groups, national shock label or crash probabilities.

### Questions readers can use

- **For a proposed local project:** How much demand is contracted, how much is operating, and who covers unused capacity if the project is delayed or cancelled?
- **For electricity bills:** Which costs are attributed to this project, which are shared system costs, and what do the approved tariff and contract actually recover?
- **For the build-out thesis:** Are connections and equipment arriving faster, and are useful workloads supporting the operating costs?
- **For the next research update:** Compare actual load, project completions and rate outcomes against dated expectations. Keep requests for grid capacity separate from operating demand.

## Executive Summary
This paper frames the AI data-center boom as a researchable economic regime rather than a simple prediction call. The recorded Forge read is strain_without_break with bubble phase reflexive_buildout. The strongest version of the thesis is that demand remains real while physical bottlenecks and financing/crowding risks create a conditional bubble dynamic. The recorded 21-window survivor/fragility test has a 0.57 hit rate and 4.20 percentage-point average spread. This is null-to-weak evidence, not demonstrated selection skill.

**Plain English:** The question is not whether AI demand is fake. The question is whether real demand is running into power, parts, financing, and regional cost limits faster than markets expect.

**Technical Read:** Treat the build-out as a bottleneck-transmission regime: scarcity rents can coexist with fragile financing, regional ratepayer pressure, project delays, and valuation overshoot.

## Research Question
Can the data-center build-out be explained by a bottleneck-transmission model that predicts which industries retain pricing power, which regions see power-cost pressure, and when bubble-like financial fragility overtakes genuine infrastructure demand?

**Plain English:** We want to separate the winners, the squeezed suppliers, the exposed regions, and the weak players before the market makes that split obvious.

**Technical Read:** The target model should estimate component bottleneck persistence, regional rate/capex transmission, financing fragility, and scenario transitions without relying on one deterministic burst date.

## Bubble Mechanics Framework
The paper separates bubble formation from burst timing. Demand can stay strong while a bubble forms if investors extrapolate scarcity profits, if second-tier participation broadens, or if financing quality degrades. Timestamped exploratory model-state outputs are: formation 0.73, psychological heat 0.57, capex-reality gap 0.67, financing fragility 0.62.

**Plain English:** Bubbles usually grow while the story still sounds right. The danger point is when expectations outrun cash flow, physical capacity, or financing quality.

**Technical Read:** The framework separates formation heat, psychological participation, capex-reality gap, financing fragility, survivor quality, and weak-player exposure. That keeps the paper from treating a broad correction and a total demand collapse as the same event.

**Plain English:** Markets can turn while demand still looks strong. The first warning is usually a change in belief: investors start asking who actually earns returns, who is overbuilding, and who needs funding. Panic risk rises when that doubt collides with weak credit or forced selling.

**Technical Read:** The psychology layer combines narrative heat, capex-reality gap, support deterioration, financing fragility, power/capex pressure, and breadth quality. The behavioral cascade then tests whether incentives and leverage have progressed from speculative expansion into refinancing stress and forced selling. It separates a valuation reset from a panic/liquidity break, which matters because the right evidence and timing signals are different.

## Financial Deflation And Margin-Normalization Risk
The paper separates physical scarcity from financial deflation. **Plain English:** A bubble does not have to end because demand disappears. It can also cool because supply catches up, margins compress, or investors stop paying for every scarce input as if scarcity will last forever.

**Technical Read:** The financial-deflation lane models pricing-power fade, crowding reset, market risk, support deterioration, and catch-up pressure separately from physical bottlenecks. It remains modeled until measured ASP, inventory, backlog, and margin data are added.
