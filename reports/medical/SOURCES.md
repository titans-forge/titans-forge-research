# Source Ledger

Edition: October 15, 2026.

Identifiers, titles and abstracts were checked against exact Europe PMC records linked to PubMed. The summaries below are original, bounded interpretations, not copied abstracts. This is a selected bibliography, not a systematic review, full-text risk-of-bias assessment, complete conflict-of-interest audit or exhaustive retraction search. Metadata identity is not a scientific-quality endorsement. The Titans Forge medical rubric has not been fully applied to every paper in this edition.

One DOI is one bibliographic work here; linked registrations are not additional results. Several works can still share participants or cohorts; this ledger does not assert independent samples. Unresolved lookups are excluded, not declared nonexistent. The JSON companion retains retrieval URLs and raw-record hashes, but no abstracts or source full text.

## S00

**Assessment of Urolithin A effects on muscle endurance, strength, inflammation, oxidative stress, and protein metabolism in male athletes with resistance training: an 8-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study.**

Zhao H, Zhu H, Yun H, Liu J, Song G, Teng J, Zou D, Lu N, Liu C. Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition. 2024-11-02.

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39487653/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/15502783.2024.2419388)

- Design: `human_randomized_trial`.
- Intervention/topic: urolithin_a.
- Population/scope: Twenty resistance-trained men, eight weeks.
- Finding: Some isometric/repetition outcomes improved; between-group one-repetition maximum results were not significant. Inflammatory measures were mixed, including a within-group CRP increase.
- Boundary: Small male athletic sample; no inference about general fatigue or brain disease.

## S01

**Effects of a mitochondrial-targeted ubiquinol on vascular function and exercise capacity in chronic kidney disease: a randomized controlled pilot study.**

Kirkman DL, Stock JM, Shenouda N, Bohmke NJ, Kim Y, Kidd J, Townsend RR, Edwards DG. American journal of physiology. Renal physiology. 2023-08-10.

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37560769/) | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajprenal.00067.2023)

- Design: `human_randomized_trial`.
- Intervention/topic: mitoq.
- Population/scope: Eighteen people with chronic kidney disease, four weeks.
- Finding: MitoQ pilot reported a flow-mediated dilation signal but no exercise-capacity benefit; the microvascular comparison was borderline (P=.053).
- Boundary: MitoQ is not PQQ or ordinary CoQ10/ubiquinol. Not direct evidence for a proposed stack component.

## S10
