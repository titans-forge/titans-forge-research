# Mito Combo Evidence Landscape

**Corrected research edition | October 15, 2026 | Research only**

## Summary

CoQ10/ubiquinol, PQQ, urolithin A (UA) and DHA have different biological rationales and different bodies of evidence. This selected literature does not establish efficacy, safety, additivity or synergy of the four-component combination. The defensible next output is a better-specified research question, not a supplement recommendation.

The earlier report's quantitative rankings and evidence-row totals are withdrawn because query-derived compound labels and misclassified study designs contaminated them. This edition uses individually identified papers and endpoint-specific descriptions instead. Bibliographic records and abstracts were checked; a full-text Titans Forge medical-rubric review remains incomplete.

## Corrections That Change the Interpretation

The kidney pilot [S01](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37560769/) used **MitoQ**, not PQQ or ordinary CoQ10. Its vascular signal is adjacent evidence only. The mitochondrial-nutrient paper [S07](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25380523/) is a review, not a completed trial of this exact combination. The MCI paper [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38908296/) tested hydrogen-producing minerals plus PQQ; its primary BDNF interaction was not significant. None establishes a four-part stack result.

The historical July 7, 2026 registry snapshot did not identify a direct four-part trial, but it was neither exhaustive nor refreshed for this publication. No universal absence-of-trials claim or current recruiting-status assertion is made.

## Highest-Value Research Questions

1. Which single population and prespecified functional endpoint are biologically justified, measurable and not already explained by exposure or training effects?
2. Does a candidate component improve its primary endpoint relative to control, not merely its own baseline or an exploratory subgroup?
3. Does any combination add benefit beyond its constituents, and is the interaction estimate precise enough to support synergy rather than simple co-administration?
4. Can safety, tolerability, adherence and formulation consistency be established in the intended population before a disease-outcome trial?

These are design priorities, not evidence-based rankings of which compound works best. A factorial or staged dismantling study could separate component effects, but complexity and interaction power must be justified prospectively.

## Claims Not Supported

This collection does not show that the combination treats fatigue, kidney disease, cognitive impairment, stroke, inflammation or aging. It provides no patient-specific treatment or dosing guidance. Hashes, source identity checks and successful package tests do not change those scientific limits.
